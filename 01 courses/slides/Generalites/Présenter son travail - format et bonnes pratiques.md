## How you're graded

- **Practice**<br>Did you put what you learned into practice? Does it work?
- **Knowledge**<br>Did you step out of your comfort zone? Learn new things?
- **Form**<br>Oral presentation, level of English

Note:
Practice is what "2 · What did you do?" shows: screenshots, a video, the
thing running. Knowledge comes out in "1 · Why?" and "3 · What did you
learn?". Form is the rest of this deck.

---

## Follow the timeline

![[presenter_quatre_questions.svg]]

Dig into each question until it fills **one slide with 3–4 bullets**.

Note:
Chronological order: the audience follows your path, from the first idea
to what comes next. Four questions, four slides, plus a title slide.

---

## 1 · Why?

Why these projects, or why these topics?

- What triggered it? A game, a course, a problem…
- What did you want to build, or understand?
- Why these one, and not something else?

---

## 2 · What did you do?

**Show it :** screenshots, videos, examples.

- Which tools and technologies?
- What were the main steps?
- What worked? What didn't?

Note:
Show, don't tell: a 20-second clip of the thing running says more than a
paragraph about it.

---

## 3 · What did you learn?

Be concrete. Dead ends count too.

- What can you do now that you couldn't before?
- What was harder than expected?
- What would you do differently?

---

## 4 · What's next?

Keep going, or move on?

- Keep going: how?
- Move on: to which topic or project?
- Either way: why?

## Slides: a support, not a script

- They help you get the words out
- Keywords on screen, sentences out loud
- Talk to the audience, not to the screen

---

## In English

- As correct as possible…
- …but, as McDonald's says: *venez comme vous êtes*
- Stuck on a word? Rephrase, and keep going

Note:
Nobody expects perfect English. What matters is that you talk.

---

## Rehearse out loud

- At least once: in your head doesn't count
- Record yourself, or present to someone
- Listen back: where do you stumble?

---

## Checklist

- One slide per question: why, what, learned, next
- Screenshots or a video of your work
- Sober colors, big text, simple layout
- Rehearsed out loud
