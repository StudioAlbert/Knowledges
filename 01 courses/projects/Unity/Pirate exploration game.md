### Genre definition

> A **beat 'em up** (also known as **brawler** and, in some markets, **beat 'em all**[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_%27em_up#cite_note-OQLF-1)) is a [video game genre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_genre) featuring [hand-to-hand combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand-to-hand_combat) against a large number of opponents. Traditional beat 'em ups take place in [scrolling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side-scrolling_video_game), [two-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2D_computer_graphics) (2D) levels, while a number of modern games feature more open [three-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_computer_graphics) (3D) environments with yet larger numbers of enemies.

The [gameplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gameplay) tends to follow [arcade genre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_genre)
 conventions, such as being simple to learn but difficult to master, and
 the combat system tends to be more highly developed than other [side-scrolling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side-scrolling) [action games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_games). Two-player [cooperative gameplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_video_game) and multiple [player characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Player_character)
 are also hallmarks of the genre. Most of these games take place in 
urban settings and feature crime-fighting and revenge-based plots, 
though some games may employ historical, science fiction or fantasy 
themes.

Popular examples of 2D Beat 'Em Up games include classics like Streets of Rage, Final Fight, and Double Dragon. This genre has seen a resurgence in recent years with modern titles like Castle Crashers, Mother Russia Bleeds, and Streets of Rage 4, which blend classic gameplay mechanics with contemporary design elements.

### Key features of 2D Beat 'Em Up games include:

1. **Side-Scrolling Perspective:** The gameplay takes place on a 2D plane, with the action unfolding horizontally. Players move their characters from one side of the screen to the other, progressing through levels or stages.
2. **Melee Combat:** Combat primarily involves close-quarters, hand-to-hand combat. Characters use punches, kicks, and other melee attacks to defeat enemies. Some games may also incorporate weapons, such as knives or bats, that characters can pick up and use temporarily.
3. **Enemy Waves:** Players face waves of enemies, often with increasing difficulty as they progress through the game. The challenge lies in dealing with multiple opponents simultaneously and employing effective combat strategies.
4. **Boss Battles:** Many 2D Beat 'Em Up games feature boss battles at the end of each level or at key points in the game. Boss characters are typically larger and more formidable than regular enemies, requiring players to employ different tactics to defeat them.
5. **Cooperative Play:** Some games in this genre support cooperative multiplayer, allowing two or more players to team up and play through the game together. Cooperative play enhances the experience as players can coordinate attacks and strategize together.
6. **Power-Ups and Items:** Players often encounter power-ups or items that enhance their characters' abilities or restore health. These can include temporary boosts to attack power, speed, or special moves.
7. **Linear Level Progression:** The game typically follows a linear level progression, with players advancing through a series of stages, each presenting new challenges and enemies. Some games may offer branching paths or hidden areas.
8. **Pixel Art and Animation:** Many 2D Beat 'Em Up games feature pixel art graphics, contributing to a retro or nostalgic aesthetic. Fluid character animations are crucial to conveying the intensity of the combat.
