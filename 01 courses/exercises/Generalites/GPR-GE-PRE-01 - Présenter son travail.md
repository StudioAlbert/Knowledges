# Exercises — GPR-GE-PRE-01 — Presenting your work

> Lecture: [[01 courses/slides/Generalites/GPR-GE-PRE-01 - Présenter son travail|GPR-GE-PRE-01 - Presenting your work]]

Take a project you actually worked on — a course assignment, a game jam entry, something you built at home. You will present it in **five slides**: a title slide, then one slide per question.

## Exercise 1 — One line per question

Before touching any slide software, write four sentences on paper. One sentence each, no more.

1. **Why?** What triggered this project or topic?
2. **What did you do?** What did you build, with which tools?
3. **What did you learn?** Name one thing you can do now that you couldn't before.
4. **What's next?** Keep going, or move on — and why?

If a sentence doesn't come, the answer isn't clear enough yet. Say it out loud to someone before writing it down.

## Exercise 2 — Grow each line into a slide

Turn each sentence into a slide with **3 to 4 bullets**. Keywords, not sentences: the full sentences are what you say out loud.

1. Write the four content slides, plus a title slide with the project name.
2. On slide 2, add the proof: a screenshot, a short clip, the thing running. No proof means no *Practice* points.
3. Strip the decoration — sober colors, big text, one idea per line. If a bullet wraps onto three lines, cut it.

## Exercise 3 — Rehearse out loud, with a timer

In your head does not count.

1. Present the five slides out loud, alone, and time yourself.
2. Note every place where you stumble or hesitate. Those are the spots where the slide doesn't match what you want to say — fix the slide, not your memory.
3. Do it a second time. Compare the two times: the second one should be shorter and steadier.

## Exercise 4 — Review a classmate's deck

Swap decks with someone. Go through their five slides with the checklist and give **one** concrete thing to change.

1. Is there one slide per question, in order?
2. Is there a screenshot or a video of the work itself?
3. Can you read the smallest text from the back of the room?
4. Does any slide carry a sentence they could say instead of show?

Then say what you would fix first, and why. Vague praise doesn't help anyone.
